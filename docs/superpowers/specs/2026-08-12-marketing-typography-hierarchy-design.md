# Marketing typography hierarchy design

## Goal

Improve readability and typographic inheritance across the marketing route
group, with the homepage as the primary surface, without changing Anthon's
visual identity, content, layout, colors, motion, or chosen typefaces.

## Scope

The shared typographic foundation applies to `src/app/(marketing)/` through its
layout. The visible hierarchy work is concentrated on the homepage components:

- hero;
- feature sections and cards;
- how-it-works steps;
- method section;
- final call to action;
- shared navbar and footer.

Pricing, help, legal, profile, and channels inherit the corrected marketing
font foundation. They receive no broader visual redesign in this pass.
Operational surfaces such as profile and channels keep their compact interface
scale and do not adopt landing-page display treatments.

## Non-goals

This work does not change:

- Barlow, Barlow Condensed, or Geist Mono as the selected families;
- copy, claims, labels, or calls to action;
- colors, borders, cards, imagery, or iconography;
- section order, alignment, structural spacing, or responsive layout;
- animation behavior;
- typography outside the marketing route group.

## Typographic foundation

Add a dedicated class to the marketing layout root. Within that scope, use the
CSS variables generated by `next/font` as the canonical font-family source:
Barlow for ordinary text, Barlow Condensed for display text, and Geist Mono for
the existing technical labels. Do not repeat literal family names in marketing
component styles when inheritance or a shared role can express the same rule.

The marketing root owns the ordinary text family and baseline line-height.
Display and mono families remain explicit semantic exceptions. Avoid broad
element selectors for `h1`, `h2`, `p`, or interactive elements so Clerk and
operational components cannot accidentally acquire landing-page styling.

## Homepage hierarchy

The homepage follows one deliberate reading order:

1. hero title;
2. section title;
3. card or step title;
4. introductory and body copy;
5. labels and microcopy.

The hero remains the only dominant display level and retains its present
responsive proportions, condensed extra-bold face, tight leading, uppercase
treatment, and yellow highlight.

All homepage section titles share one display role: Barlow Condensed, bold,
uppercase, tight tracking, and consistent line-height at the current visual
scale. Existing section alignment stays unchanged.

Card and step titles share one subordinate display role. Their size,
line-height, tracking, and weight remain visibly below section titles even in
uppercase. The role removes the current variation between semibold and bold
cards and prevents cards from competing with their parent section.

Introductory copy shares a readable lead role with consistent size and relaxed
line-height. Card descriptions share a body role with a slightly more compact
scale but the same readable rhythm. Existing line-length constraints remain;
this pass does not resize containers or restructure grids.

Existing eyebrow and technical-label treatments use a common small-text role.
Keep the mono and uppercase character, but avoid undersized type and excessive
letter spacing: the target floor is 12px with tracking no wider than `0.16em`
unless a component has a documented functional constraint.

Buttons and navigation retain their current appearance and sizing. They inherit
the marketing text family and declare weight only when it communicates action
hierarchy.

## Shared roles and inheritance

Implement a small scoped vocabulary rather than React typography wrappers. It
must cover:

- the display hierarchy, with hero, section, and card levels;
- lead and body copy;
- eyebrow or kicker text;
- compact navigation and microcopy.

Shared roles own family, weight, line-height, and tracking. Components keep only
genuine exceptions such as responsive size changes, color, alignment, or the
hero highlight geometry. This keeps markup recognizable while removing
duplicated combinations of typographic utility classes.

The shared vocabulary must remain scoped below the marketing root. It must not
alter chat, admin, authentication, or dashboard typography.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

- Body copy remains at least 16px on the homepage with a line-height of at
  least 1.5.
- Small uppercase labels remain at least 12px and use reduced tracking for
  legibility.
- Italian headings must wrap without clipping or isolated overflow at mobile
  widths.
- Font weight and size, not color alone, communicate hierarchy.
- Light and dark themes preserve the same hierarchy and readable wrapping.
- Existing reduced-motion behavior remains unchanged.

## Verification

Verify the homepage in a running Next.js application at representative desktop
and mobile viewports in both light and dark themes. Check the reading order,
heading wraps, line-height, uppercase readability, card-title subordination,
and navbar/footer consistency. Confirm that pricing, help, legal, profile, and
channels still inherit Barlow without adopting homepage display roles.

Run:

1. focused Biome checks for every changed marketing or global-style file;
2. homepage and marketing tests affected by class changes;
3. the relevant unit suite;
4. `git diff --check`;
5. the Impeccable mechanical detector once over the finished UI targets;
6. Next.js runtime compilation and error checks plus browser inspection.

## Acceptance criteria

- The marketing route group has one explicit, scoped font-inheritance root.
- Marketing font families resolve through the variables generated by
  `next/font`.
- The homepage visibly reads as hero, section, card, body, then microcopy.
- Repeated homepage levels use consistent family, weight, leading, and
  tracking.
- Small uppercase labels are no smaller than 12px and no wider than `0.16em`
  tracking without a documented exception.
- Other marketing pages inherit the corrected base but retain their current
  layouts and interface-specific scale.
- Chat, admin, authentication, and dashboard typography remain unchanged.
- Copy, palette, structure, motion, and selected typefaces are unchanged.
- Automated checks and bounded desktop/mobile runtime verification pass, or an
  environment-only unavailable check is reported with precise substitute
  evidence.
